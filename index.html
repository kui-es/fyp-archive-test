  1. FYP-20-24Design and Fabrication of an Automated Peanut Shelling Machine

    Abstract

    Peanuts are a widely consumed nut in Kenya. However, majority of the processing done on nuts requires their skins to be removed, making the need for an efficient de-skinning process essential for productivity. The existing traditional method of deskinning/peeling peanuts, involving manually rubbing the peanuts between two abrasive surfaces, is both time-consuming and low in productivity. To overcome these challenges, an automated peanut peeling machine is designed and fabricated.

    The machine incorporates a hopper with a capacity to hold 4.4kg of peanuts and feed them into a shelling mechanism. This mechanism features a rotating inner drum, which is fitted with polypropylene brushes. These brushes peel the peanuts as they are pressed against the inner surface of an enclosing drum lined with rubber abrasive. A blower fan winnows the skins off to a separate outlet. The peeled peanuts are then assessed via TCS3472 colour sensors that separate fully peeled peanuts from unpeeled ones. The entire system is controlled by an Arduino Mega microcontroller. This automated approach saves time, increases productivity, and improves the consistency of peeled peanuts compared to manual methods. The peeling efficiency of the machine is approximately 90%.

  2. FYP-21-6Design and Fabrication of an Automated Maize Seed Planter with Precision Spacing

    Abstract

    Maize is the staple crop for most Kenyan households, yet many smallholders still plant by hand, giving uneven spacing and low germination. This project designs and fabricates a push-type maize planter that places seeds at a set spacing regardless of how fast the operator walks. A wheel encoder measures the distance travelled and an Arduino Nano turns a seed metering disc through a stepper motor so that one seed is dropped at every 25 cm.

    The planter also opens a furrow to a depth adjustable between 3 and 7 cm and covers the seed. In field tests on a 100 m row, 97% of seeds were placed within 3 cm of the target spacing, compared with 62% for hand planting, and the planter covered 0.1 hectare in about 50 minutes.

  1. FYP-17-4Smart Door Access Control System Using Facial Recognition

    Abstract

    Key and card based access control is easily defeated by lost or shared credentials. This project develops a door access control system that unlocks for registered users after recognising their face. A Raspberry Pi with a camera module detects faces, extracts embeddings with a lightweight neural network and compares them with a stored database of enrolled users.

    When a match is found the controller energises an electric door strike for five seconds and logs the event with a timestamp; unknown faces trigger a buzzer and a photo is saved for review. A motion sensor wakes the system to save power. On a test set of 20 enrolled users the system recognised the correct person 96% of the time in normal indoor lighting, with a response time of 1.3 seconds.

  2. FYP-17-15Design and Fabrication of a Voice-Controlled Wheelchair with Obstacle Avoidance

    Abstract

    Many people with limited arm mobility cannot operate a conventional joystick-driven powered wheelchair. This project converts a manual wheelchair into a powered one that responds to spoken commands such as forward, left, right and stop, while a ring of ultrasonic sensors prevents collisions. A voice recognition module is trained on the user's voice, and an Arduino maps recognised commands to speed and direction commands for two 250 W hub motors.

    The controller reduces speed as an obstacle comes within 1.5 m and stops the chair at 0.5 m, regardless of the voice command. A joystick and an emergency stop button are retained as overrides. In indoor trials the command recognition rate was 89% in a quiet room and 71% with background noise, and the chair stopped before every obstacle in 30 approach tests.

  3. FYP-19-8Design and Fabrication of a GPS-Based Dual Axis Solar Tracker

    Abstract

    This project presents the successful design and fabrication of a GPS-based dual-axis solar tracker, a cutting-edge technology aimed at increasing energy capture and optimizing solar panel efficiency by dynamically adjusting their orientation throughout the day. The tracker incorporates a Global Positioning System (GPS) module to accurately determine the sun’s position, enabling precise alignment of solar panels for maximum energy capture. The dual-axis functionality allows the system to track both the azimuth and elevation angles, ensuring optimal alignment with the sun’s position in real time.

    The fabrication process involved the integration of high-precision motors, sensors, and a microcontroller to achieve seamless and automated solar panel adjustments. The completed project showcases a reliable and sustainable solution to enhance the performance of solar energy systems, contributing to increased renewable energy utilization in various applications.

    The dual-axis solar tracker system demonstrates superior adaptability to changing environmental conditions, ensuring its robust functionality under varying weather and geographical circumstances. The integration of GPS technology enables the tracker to operate autonomously without the need for manual intervention, enhancing its efficiency and practicality for deployment in diverse locations. The successful implementation of the project underscores its potential for widespread adoption in solar power generation, offering a scalable and eco-friendly solution to boost the overall output and reliability of solar energy systems. This innovative design marks a significant step towards advancing solar technology, addressing the challenges of energy sustainability in an increasingly demanding global landscape.

  1. FYP-14-12Arduino-Based Fire Detection and Suppression Robot

    Abstract

    Fires in storage rooms and small factories can grow quickly before people are able to respond, and sending a person into a smoke-filled room is dangerous. This project presents a small tracked robot that detects a fire, navigates towards it and extinguishes it with a water spray, while sending an alert to a nearby responder. The robot carries three flame sensors that give the direction of the fire, a gas sensor that detects smoke, and a temperature sensor used to confirm that the fire is out.

    The tracked chassis was selected after comparing wheeled and tracked layouts on loose and smooth floors, and was built from aluminium sheet and 3D-printed parts. A servo-mounted nozzle is aimed at the flame using the sensor readings, and a submersible pump delivers water from a 1.5 litre tank. A GSM module sends an SMS alert when a fire is detected and again when it has been extinguished.

    In tests with a small paraffin fire placed 2 m from the robot, the fire was located and put out in an average of 24 seconds across ten trials, with one failure caused by a reflection from a metal surface being read as a flame. The report recommends adding a thermal camera and a larger tank before the design is used outside the laboratory.

  2. FYP-14-20Automatic Coffee Cherry Sorting Machine Using Colour Sensing

    Abstract

    The quality of coffee depends strongly on picking only ripe cherries, yet smallholder farmers often deliver a mixture of ripe, unripe and overripe fruit. This project designs and fabricates a machine that sorts coffee cherries by colour. Cherries are singulated by a vibratory feeder and carried along a conveyor belt, where colour sensors classify each cherry as ripe (red) or reject (green or dark).

    A short pulse from an air nozzle diverts rejects into a separate bin. The machine is controlled by an Arduino Mega and handles about 18 kg of cherries per hour. In tests on mixed samples of 500 cherries the sorting accuracy was 91%, compared with 78% for manual sorting by trained workers over the same time.

  1. FYP-07-9Autonomous Line-Following Vehicle for Warehouse Material Handling

    Abstract

    Moving goods between racks and dispatch bays in small warehouses is still largely done by hand. This project designs and builds an autonomous line-following vehicle that carries loads of up to 20 kg along a marked floor route. An array of eight infrared sensors detects the guide line, and a PID controller running on an Arduino adjusts the speed of two DC gear motors to keep the vehicle centred on the line.

    An ultrasonic sensor stops the vehicle when an obstacle is detected within 40 cm, and RFID tags embedded at stations let it stop and wait for loading. Over 50 test runs on a 30 m course the vehicle completed 94% of runs without leaving the line, at an average speed of 0.5 m/s.

  2. FYP-11-3Design of a Solar-Powered Automated Poultry Feeder

    Abstract

    Feeding poultry by hand at fixed times is labour-intensive and leads to uneven feeding and wasted feed. This project describes the design and fabrication of an automated poultry feeder powered by a solar panel and battery, suitable for farms with unreliable grid supply. A hopper stores feed, and a motor-driven auger dispenses a measured quantity at times set by the farmer through a keypad and LCD.

    A load cell measures the feed dispensed, and the controller stops the auger when the target mass is reached, with an alarm when the hopper is nearly empty. Calibration tests showed a dispensing error of 3.5% against the set mass. The system ran for three cloudy days on battery alone, and the cost of the prototype was KES 22,500.

  1. FYP-02-11Design and Fabrication of an Automated Vertical Hydroponic Farming System

    Abstract

    Land scarcity and unpredictable rainfall limit vegetable production for urban households in Kenya. This project presents the design and fabrication of an automated vertical hydroponic system that grows leafy vegetables in a small footprint using the nutrient film technique. A microcontroller reads pH, electrical conductivity, water temperature and water level sensors, and doses nutrient and pH-adjusting solutions through peristaltic pumps to keep the solution within set limits. Readings are sent over Wi-Fi to a mobile dashboard where the grower can view trends and receive alerts.

    In a four-week trial with kale seedlings, the system held the pH within 0.3 of the setpoint and used about 80% less water than soil cultivation of the same crop. The prototype cost KES 38,000 to build, and the report outlines how the design can be scaled for small commercial growers.

  2. FYP-02-14Design and Fabrication of a Low-Cost Three-Degree-of-Freedom Robotic Arm for Small Workshops

    Abstract

    Small workshops in Kenya rely on manual handling for repetitive pick-and-place tasks, which is slow and tiring for operators. This project designs and fabricates a low-cost three-degree-of-freedom robotic arm that can be programmed for such tasks. The arm is built from laser-cut mild steel and 3D-printed PLA joints, driven by three stepper motors with belt reduction and a servo-actuated parallel gripper.

    Forward and inverse kinematics were derived and implemented on an Arduino Mega, and a simple teach-pendant interface lets the operator record and replay positions. Tests with a 250 g payload gave a repeatability of 1.2 mm and a cycle time of 6.5 seconds for a 300 mm transfer. The arm cost under KES 45,000 compared with imported industrial units priced many times higher.

  3. FYP-7-5Smart Irrigation Controller Using Soil Moisture Sensing and LoRa Communication

    Abstract

    Smallholder farmers often irrigate on a fixed schedule, wasting water when the soil is already moist and under-watering during dry spells. This project presents a soil-moisture-based irrigation controller in which field nodes measure moisture at the root zone and report to a central controller over a LoRa radio link, removing the need for mobile network coverage on the farm.

    The controller opens solenoid valves when moisture falls below a crop-specific threshold and closes them once the target is reached. Field nodes are powered by a small solar panel and battery. Range tests gave reliable communication at 1.4 km line of sight, and a two-week trial on a tomato plot reduced water use by 37% compared with scheduled irrigation without any measurable drop in yield indicators.
